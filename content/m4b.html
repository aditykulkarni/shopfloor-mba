New technology does not spread evenly. It moves through groups of buyers who want different things, and many products stall between two of those groups.

The whole product

What the customer needs is more than what the vendor ships. The core product is the software or machine. The whole product is everything required for the customer to get the promised result: integration, data migration, training, templates, support, and partners who can implement. A strong core product with a weak whole product fails in the mainstream market.

The adoption lifecycle

GroupShareWhat they want
InnovatorsVery smallThe technology itself. Will tolerate rough edges.
Early adopters (visionaries)SmallA breakthrough ahead of competitors. Will fund a project.
Early majority (pragmatists)About a thirdA proven solution with references from firms like theirs.
Late majorityAbout a thirdA standard, low-risk, low-effort purchase.
LaggardsThe restTo be left alone.

The chasm

Geoffrey Moore's observation: visionaries and pragmatists are so different that success with the first group does not carry over to the second. Visionaries buy a promise. Pragmatists buy what their peers have bought, and a visionary's custom project is no reference for them. Between the two groups there is a gap in which many technology products die.

The way across is to pick one narrow segment of pragmatists with an urgent problem, build the complete solution for that segment, dominate it, and use it as the reference to enter the next. Moore calls it a beachhead.

The product life cycle

Markets move through introduction, growth, maturity and decline. Priorities change with the stage: educate the market in introduction, win share in growth, defend margin and segment more finely in maturity, harvest or exit in decline. A mature category can be renewed by a new technology wave, which restarts the adoption curve.

Applying it

Virtual commissioning, digital twins and AI-based planning have each been through visionary projects at large manufacturers. Their mainstream adoption depends on packaged, low-risk versions with references, and that is a whole-product problem, not a technology problem.

In your job

Work out which kind of buyer you are facing. A visionary wants to hear what becomes possible. A pragmatist wants to hear who else like them has done it, how long it took and what went wrong. The same demo shown to both will miss one of them.

Pragmatist buyers in the early majority rely most on:

  1. The newest features
  2. References from similar companies
  3. The lowest price

They want proof that it works for firms like theirs.

The "whole product" includes:

  1. Only the software licence
  2. Only the features on the roadmap
  3. Everything the customer needs to get the result, including services and partners

Mainstream buyers purchase outcomes.

Moore's advice for crossing the chasm is to:

  1. Dominate one narrow segment first
  2. Sell to as many industries as possible
  3. Cut prices sharply

A beachhead produces the references pragmatists need.

The economics

A field sales person might cost ₹1.2 crore a year including salary, commission, travel and pre-sales support. With a quota of ₹8 crore, the cost of sale is 15%. That person cannot profitably chase deals of ₹10 lakh, because twenty calls on a small deal cost more than the deal earns.

A reseller typically keeps 20 to 40% of the price as margin. For that, the vendor gets coverage it could not afford directly. A simple rule: the lower the deal size and the wider the market, the more the channel should be indirect.

What partners need

A partner is an independent business. It will put effort where it earns most with least trouble. To get that effort a vendor must offer adequate margin, protection from being undercut by the vendor's own sales team, training, leads, and a product that customers ask for. Partners often make more from services around a product than from reselling it.

Channel conflict

Conflict arises when two channels chase the same customer, or when the vendor takes a partner's best account direct. Rules of engagement set who serves which customers by size, industry or named-account list, and how each side is paid when both contribute. Applying those rules consistently matters more than their exact design.

Channels in industrial software and machinery

Large accounts are usually served directly. Smaller ones go through value-added resellers, who also implement. Global system integrators influence the biggest programmes. Machine builders often use agents and distributors abroad, with direct service engineers for commissioning.

In your job

If you move to a firm that is a vendor's partner, the economics invert. Services margin and utilisation become the business, and the software margin is an extra. Understand how each side of the ecosystem you work in makes its money.

The funnel

Buyers move through stages: awareness, interest, evaluation, purchase. At each stage some drop out. Measuring conversion between stages shows where effort is being wasted.

How business buyers actually find suppliers

  • Peers and references, by a wide margin.
  • Their own research before any sales contact: websites, analyst reports, trade press, events.
  • Consultants and integrators.
  • Existing supplier relationships.

  3. What does the reseller route cost Meridian that the numbers do not show?

A customer is an investment. You pay to win them and they pay you back over time. Two numbers tell you whether the investment is a good one.

Customer acquisition cost

CAC = total sales and marketing cost in a period ÷ new customers won in that period

Customer lifetime value

For a subscription business with steady churn:

LTV = annual revenue per customer × gross margin ÷ annual churn rate

Churn is the share of customers who leave each year. Its inverse is the average customer life: 10% churn means an average of ten years.

Worked example

A customer pays ₹12 lakh a year. Gross margin is 80%. Churn is 10% a year. CAC is ₹30 lakh.

  • Gross profit per customer per year: ₹9.6 lakh.
  • LTV = 9.6 ÷ 0.10 = ₹96 lakh.
  • LTV ÷ CAC = 3.2.
  • CAC payback = 30 ÷ 9.6 = 3.1 years.

Rules of thumb

  • An LTV to CAC ratio of about 3 or more is widely treated as healthy. Below 1, every sale loses money.
  • Payback of 12 to 18 months is the usual aim for products sold to smaller firms. Enterprise products with very low churn accept longer.

These are conventions, not laws. They come from the present value logic of Month 3: money spent today to earn a stream of future margins.

Why churn dominates

Cut churn from 10% to 5% and LTV doubles to ₹192 lakh with nothing else changed. Raise it to 20% and LTV halves. No other lever is as strong, which is why subscription companies invest in customer success teams.

Caveats

  • The simple formula ignores discounting. Applying a discount rate lowers LTV, more so when churn is low.
  • Averages hide segments. Small customers often churn several times faster than large ones.
  • Early churn figures in a young business are unreliable.

In your job

These measures explain a vendor's behaviour towards its customers: why renewals get executive attention, why adoption after the sale is tracked, and why an account that bought and never deployed is treated as a risk. In a subscription model, delivery quality is revenue.

Annual recurring revenue

ARR is the yearly value of all active subscriptions. It differs from recognised revenue and from cash billed. It measures the size of the base that renews.

The ARR bridge

₹ croreAmount
Opening ARR100
Expansion from existing customers15
Contraction (downgrades)(2)
Churn (lost customers)(8)
ARR from customers present at the start105
New customers20
Closing ARR125

Retention measures

  • Gross revenue retention: what is left of opening ARR after churn and contraction, ignoring expansion. Here (100 − 2 − 8) ÷ 100 = 90%. It can never exceed 100%.
  • Net revenue retention (NRR): the same, including expansion. Here 105%. Above 100% means the existing base grows without any new customers.

NRR above 110% is considered strong for enterprise software. A company with NRR of 120% doubles its revenue from existing customers in about four years.

Growth against profit

The rule of 40 says that revenue growth rate plus profit margin should total at least 40. A firm growing 25% with an 18% margin scores 43. One growing 10% with a 15% margin scores 25. It is a rough test of whether growth is being bought at too high a price.

Cohorts

Group customers by the period in which they joined and track each group's revenue over time. Cohort tables show whether newer customers behave better or worse than older ones. Aggregate figures hide this while new sales are large.

Bookings, billings, revenue

Bookings are contracts signed. Billings are invoices issued. Revenue is recognised as service is delivered. A three-year deal billed annually is one booking, three billings and thirty-six months of revenue. Sales teams are paid on bookings, finance reports revenue, and the difference causes many arguments.

In your job

Sales is the part of a company most senior managers come from or answer to. It is managed with a small set of numbers.

Pipeline

The pipeline is the list of open opportunities with their value and stage. Three things describe it.

  • Coverage. Pipeline value divided by the target. With a 25% win rate, a quota of ₹6 crore needs ₹24 crore of pipeline, a coverage of four times.
  • Velocity. How quickly the pipeline turns into revenue.
  • Quality. Whether the opportunities are real: is there an economic buyer, a budget, a deadline?

Sales velocity = number of opportunities × average deal size × win rate ÷ sales cycle length

With 40 opportunities, an average deal of ₹25 lakh, a 25% win rate and a 180-day cycle: 40 × 25 × 0.25 ÷ 180 = ₹1.39 lakh a day, about ₹5 crore a year. The formula has four levers. Shortening the cycle by a third has the same effect as raising the win rate by half.

Forecasting

The usual method weights each deal by a probability for its stage and adds them up. It is expected value from Month 2, and it has the same weakness: with a few large deals the outcome is lumpy. Good forecasters judge the largest deals one by one on evidence, such as whether the customer has taken a concrete step, and not on the sales person's optimism.

Quotas and pay

A typical plan pays a base salary plus commission at target, often split around half and half for field sales, with higher rates above quota. What is paid for is what gets done:

  • Paid on revenue alone: discounts rise.
  • Paid on new customers alone: existing customers are neglected.
  • Paid on the quarter: deals get pulled forward at a cost.

Designing pay is designing behaviour. Month 7 returns to incentives.

Roles

Modern B2B sales teams divide the work: development representatives create opportunities, account executives close them, solution consultants provide technical proof, and customer success managers handle adoption and renewal. Each role has its own measure. Pre-sales is often measured on technical win rate and on the revenue it supports.
